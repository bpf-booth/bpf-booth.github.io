    The flower

    A blue that carries memory

    Clitoria ternatea · South & Southeast Asia

    For centuries, butterfly pea has been steeped, eaten and dyed with across South and Southeast Asia. In Thailand it is poured as a blue tea that blushes pink with a squeeze of lime. In Malaysian and Peranakan kitchens it colors rice and sweets made for prayer and remembrance. Ayurveda keeps it as a gentle tonic for memory and calm.

    Why it works

    The chemistry of a hidden note

    The blue comes from ternatins, anthocyanin pigments in the petals whose structure shifts with pH. Each shape absorbs light differently, so the tea changes color as acidity changes.

    At neutral pH the tea reads blue. Add acid and it turns violet, then pink. A base such as baking soda pushes it toward green.

    Our ink is clear citric acid. Where it sits on the paper, the tea turns pink, and everywhere else stays blue.
